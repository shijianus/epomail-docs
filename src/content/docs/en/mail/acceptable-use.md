# Acceptable Use Policy

**Effective date: September 29, 2026 | Version: 4.1**

This Policy gives specific effect to Section 7 ("Acceptable Use") of the [Terms of Service](/en/mail/terms-of-service/) and sets the boundaries of conduct when you use the EpoCanvas Mail service (the "Service"). Where this Policy is violated, the Operator may take measures under the "Enforcement Measures" section; conduct suspected of being criminal will also be handled according to law.

The statutes cited in this Policy refer to the versions currently in force as published in the National Laws and Regulations Database (law.moj.gov.tw); the article numbers and penalties noted are given to make the legal characterization of the conduct easier to understand and do not constitute legal advice.

## 1. Unlawful and Harmful Content

You may not use the Service to transmit, store, or distribute the following content:

| Prohibited conduct | Corresponding statutory sources |
| --- | --- |
| Child and youth sexual exploitation content, including photographing, manufacturing, reproducing, possessing, distributing, broadcasting, delivering, publicly displaying, or selling sexual images of children and youths, or pictures, audio recordings, or items of a sexual nature involving them | Child and Youth Sexual Exploitation Prevention Act, Article 2 (definition of sexual exploitation), Article 36 (photographing, manufacturing, or reproducing sexual images of children and youths without cause: imprisonment for not less than one year and not more than seven years), Article 38 (distributing, broadcasting, delivering, or publicly displaying: imprisonment for not less than one year and not more than seven years, and in addition a fine of not more than NT$5 million), Article 39 (punishment of possession) |
| Distributing or broadcasting to children and youths violent, gory, pornographic, obscene, or gambling content harmful to their physical or mental health | Act for the Protection of Children and Youths' Welfare and Rights, Article 43, Paragraph 4 (no person may distribute or broadcast such content to children and youths), Article 46 (protective obligations of internet platform providers: adopting self-regulatory rules and, upon notification, restricting access or removing the content first) |
| Recording or reproducing non-consensual intimate images, distributing them, or manufacturing fabricated intimate images by computer synthesis or other means (see Section 2) | Criminal Code, Articles 319-1 to 319-4 (the chapter on offenses against sexual privacy and fabricated intimate images); Sexual Assault Crime Prevention Act, Article 13 (removal obligations of platform operators) |
| Distributing, broadcasting, selling, or publicly displaying obscene text, pictures, audio or video recordings, or other items | Criminal Code, Article 235 (offense of distributing, broadcasting, or selling obscene materials: imprisonment for not more than two years, detention, or a fine of not more than NT$90,000, or both) |
| Email and content committing fraud, phishing, or impersonation (including forging the sender or impersonating government agencies and public officials) | Criminal Code, Article 339 (fraud: imprisonment for not more than five years, detention, or a fine of not more than NT$500,000, or both), Article 339-4 (aggravated fraud: impersonating a government agency or public official, distributing to the public through the internet or other media of communication, or committing the offense by computer synthesis: imprisonment for not less than one year and not more than seven years); Anti-Fraud Act, Articles 30, 32, and 33 (obligations to verify fraud-related content and to suspend publication, take it down and delete it, and restrict access first) |
| Distributing malware, viruses, or ransomware; email aimed at stealing credentials; unauthorized intrusion, obtaining, deleting, or altering electromagnetic records, or interfering with computer systems without cause | Criminal Code, Articles 358 to 362 (chapter on offenses against computer use: unauthorized intrusion, up to three years of imprisonment; obtaining, deleting, or altering electromagnetic records causing harm, up to five years; interfering with computer systems without cause causing harm, up to three years; creating a computer program specifically for committing offenses in that chapter and supplying it for that use, up to five years) |
| Content infringing another person's copyright, trademark rights, privacy, or reputation | The Copyright Act and the Trademark Act; the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA); Civil Code, Article 184 (tortious liability) and Article 195 (non-pecuniary damages for unlawful infringement of privacy and other personality interests) |

## 2. Non-Consensual Intimate Images

If non-consensual intimate images are uploaded, transmitted, or stored through the Service, the Operator will restrict access to or remove them upon discovery, and will handle the matter as follows:

1. **Criminal prohibitions**: recording, reproducing, distributing, broadcasting, delivering, or publicly displaying another person's intimate images without consent, or manufacturing and distributing fabricated intimate images by computer synthesis or other technological means, constitutes offenses under Article 319-1, Article 319-2, Article 319-3, and Article 319-4 of the Criminal Code, respectively.
2. **Platform removal obligation**: under Article 13 of the Sexual Assault Crime Prevention Act, internet platform providers, internet application service providers, and internet access service providers that become aware of facts suggesting a sexual assault offense **shall first restrict access to or remove** web page data related to the offense; such data, together with the suspect's personal data and network usage records, **shall be retained for 180 days** for investigation by the judicial and police authorities.
3. **Handling**: upon notification or a report, the Operator first restricts access to or removes the content, preserves evidence for the statutory period described above, and cooperates with investigations by the judicial police authorities. The handling under this Section is not subject to the appeal buffer procedure in Section 7.

## 3. Spam and Abusive Conduct

You may not do any of the following:

1. sending unsolicited bulk commercial email (UBE/UCE), marketing to recipients who have not consented, or using the Service for mailbox warm-up or address verification bombing. Taiwan's current legal system contains no dedicated anti-spam statute, but the foregoing conduct may still constitute the offense of interference with computer equipment under Article 360 of the Criminal Code or the tort under Article 184 of the Civil Code, and may violate the anti-spam laws of the recipient's jurisdiction (such as the US CAN-SPAM Act, the EU ePrivacy Directive, and Canada's CASL); for cross-border transmission, the law of the place of receipt governs. Where personal data is used for marketing, then under Article 20, Paragraph 2, of the PDPA, use of the personal data for marketing shall cease immediately when the data subject indicates refusal to accept marketing.
2. registering accounts in batches through programmatic means, bypassing human verification (Turnstile), or bypassing registration keys or quota limits.
3. using the Service as an anonymous forwarding relay or a short-lived throwaway spam pool, or repeatedly registering for the purpose of evading enforcement.

## 4. Attacks and Interference

You may not:

1. scan, probe, or brute-force the Service or third-party systems, or attempt unauthorized access to other people's mailboxes, the admin interface, or other users' data. The foregoing may constitute offenses under Article 358 to Article 360 of the Criminal Code; under Article 363 of the same Code, offenses in that chapter are prosecutable only upon complaint—prosecution proceeds once the injured party files a complaint, and the Operator may also pursue compensation and handling through civil and administrative channels.
2. use shared resources such as AI translation, attachments, or the API without legitimate reason in a manner that impedes other users' normal use (which may constitute the offense of interference under Article 360 of the Criminal Code).
3. engage in harassment, defamation, or abuse of rights against Cloudflare, the upstream open source community, or the Operator.
4. violate the terms of use of third-party services (Cloudflare, Resend, Mailjet, Telegram, and others).

## 5. General Obligations on Resource Use

1. You shall use resources within your account's storage quota; quotas are set by the Operator according to role.
2. Accounts and API tokens may not be lent, rented, or transferred to third parties for use.
3. If you bring your own external storage (BYOS) or external databases, you must yourself ensure that those services satisfy the requirements for international transfers in Article 21 of the PDPA.

## 6. Enforcement Measures

The Operator takes measures proportionate to the nature and severity of the violation; for child and youth sexual exploitation content, the graduated ladder does not apply, and the zero-tolerance procedure applies directly.

![EpoCanvas Mail enforcement ladder of the Acceptable Use Policy: five levels of warning, rate limiting, quarantine into spam, account suspension, and physical deletion, with an appeal path, and a zero-tolerance shortcut for child and youth sexual exploitation leading directly to deletion](/images/mail/aup-ladder.svg)

*Figure: The enforcement ladder. Measures escalate level by level on the principle of proportionality; physical deletion is the irreversible final measure. The zero-tolerance path shown by the dashed line applies to child and youth sexual exploitation and non-consensual intimate images, which are deleted outright with evidence preserved.*

| Level | Measure | Applicable circumstances |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Warning | Minor or first-time violations |
| 2 | Feature rate limiting | Limits on sending volume and API frequency |
| 3 | Quarantine | Email sent off-site is routed to the spam folder |
| 4 | Account suspension | Account access suspended, data retained pending review |
| 5 | Physical deletion | Account and all data deleted, irrecoverable |

Where unlawful conduct is involved, the Operator may preserve necessary evidence (in the case of the 180-day retention required by Article 13 of the Sexual Assault Crime Prevention Act, that provision governs) and cooperate with investigations by the judicial police or prosecutorial authorities according to law. If your conduct causes the Operator to be penalized by Cloudflare or an upstream provider, the Operator may seek compensation from you under Section 10 of the [Terms of Service](/en/mail/terms-of-service/).

## 7. Appeals and Reports

1. **Appeals**: if you believe an enforcement action was mistaken, you may appeal through the channels listed in Section 13 of the [Terms of Service](/en/mail/terms-of-service/); the Operator shall review and respond within a reasonable period. Except in urgent circumstances, an opportunity to explain shall be given before major enforcement actions at level 4 or above.
2. **Reports**: anyone who discovers conduct violating this Policy or a security risk (spam sources, phishing email, unauthorized access attempts, and the like) may report it through the channels described above; good-faith reports will all be verified and handled.
3. **Exception**: for content constituting the sexual exploitation defined in Article 2 of the Child and Youth Sexual Exploitation Prevention Act, or the non-consensual intimate images described in Section 2, the Operator does not apply the appeal buffer procedure; it deletes the content and terminates the account directly, preserves evidence, and refers the matter to the competent authority or the judicial police authorities under the respective statutes.
